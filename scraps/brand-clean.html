01 — Colour

Six colours, six jobs

Think of these six colours as six different tools in a mechanic's box — each one only comes out for its own job. Here's where every colour is used, and why, explained simply.

Soft Teal#5A9FA3
The colour of the brand. Used for anything the person should notice or click — links, active buttons, the little dots in the "connected stores" graphic. If a colour is "SpareCare's colour" in someone's head, it's this one.
Deep Teal#3B616C
A darker, more serious version of the teal above. Used for big headlines, the main "Talk to us" button, and any section with a dark background. This is the colour that says "this is important."
Stone Grey#99959B
A quiet, in-between grey. Used for regular paragraph text, small captions, and outline buttons that aren't the main action on the page — things that should be readable but not shout for attention.
Cloud Grey#E5E5E6
The lightest colour in the set. Used as the background colour of the whole page, and for thin dividing lines between sections — like the wall behind everything else, never the main attraction.
Ink Black#040101
The colour of ordinary reading text — the closest thing to "black" a person will actually read, on things like paragraphs and part descriptions.
Onyx#030000
A true, solid black. Used only for large dark surfaces — the dark version of the logo, a dark footer — never for text, since Ink Black already does that job.

One exception: the green, amber and red used on stock tags (further down this page) are kept outside this palette on purpose. Everyone already reads green as "good," amber as "careful," and red as "stop" — like a traffic light — so those three stay standard no matter what the brand colours are.

03 — Logo

The gear gives way to the hand

The mark reads as a gear opening into a cupped hand — the mechanical and the caretaking sides of the same business. Keep the two elements intact and in proportion; never separate the hand from the ring.

Minimum clear space on every side equals the height of the gear's outer tooth. Minimum display size: 24px tall in digital contexts, 12mm in print.
Don't stretch or distort
Don't recolour off-palette
Don't add drop shadows
Don't rotate or tilt

06 — Gradient

Used for depth, never for decoration

Gradients appear only on large surfaces standing in for metal or motion — a hero panel, a loading state — always moving from teal toward its darker or lighter neighbour, never rainbowed.

Deep Teal → Soft Teal. The main hero and dark-panel gradient — think of it as the teal "waking up."
Stone Grey → Cloud Grey. For metal-looking surfaces and quiet dividers, like brushed steel.
Deep Teal → Onyx, used once per screen at most, for a single moment of real weight — a dark hero panel or a full-screen takeover, never a small button.
07 — Voice

Talk like the counter, not the boardroom

SpareCare's audience is store owners and parts managers, not procurement executives. Write the way you'd talk across a counter: plain, specific, and quick to the point.

Do

  • "3 stores near you already have this part."
  • "Your Bay-04 stock hasn't synced since 6pm."
  • Name real parts, brands, and store names in examples.
  • Say what happened and what to do next.

Don't

  • "Leverage real-time inventory intelligence."
  • "Unlock synergies across your supply network."
  • Use jargon a counter clerk wouldn't use out loud.
  • Apologise or hedge in error messages — just explain.

The rules the WMS screens are drawn to

Every colour, size, corner and word in the Inward wireframes comes from this document. It exists so that two people drawing two different screens six months apart produce something that looks like one product. Each section below says what the rule is in plain language, and then names the screen where you can go and see it.

01 — COLOUR

Six colours, six jobs

The brand runs on six colours and nothing else. Two teals carry the brand, two greys carry the background, and two near-blacks carry the text. Anything that looks like a seventh colour on a screen is either a signal colour (section 02) or a mistake.

Soft Teal#5A9FA3The colour of the brand. Used for the active state, the focus ring and anything the eye should go to first.
Deep Teal#3B616CThe serious version. Headings, primary buttons, and every number the operator is meant to read.
Stone Grey#99959BThe in-between grey. Secondary button borders and anything deliberately quiet.
Cloud Grey#E5E5E6The lightest. Page background and disabled controls.
Ink Black#040101Ordinary reading text. Not pure black — pure black on a bright screen is tiring at a dock.
Onyx#030000Used once per screen at most, for a single moment of weight.
What this means in practice

A designer never picks a colour. They pick a job — "this is a heading", "this is a background" — and the job already has a colour attached to it. That is why the whole Inward module can be recoloured for a different dealer by changing six values.

Where to see it

Screen 3, Inward Operations Dashboard. The page sits on Cloud Grey, the cards are white, the KPI numbers are Deep Teal, and the active queue tab is Soft Teal. Four of the six colours, doing four different jobs, on one screen.

02 — SIGNAL COLOURS

Green, amber and red are not brand colours

Green, amber and red sit deliberately outside the six. They are a traffic light. A warehouse operator reads them without thinking, and that reflex is worth more than brand consistency — so they are never re-tinted to match the teal.

Green#4C9A6AThe box is OK. Nothing to do.
Amber#E2963CLook at this. A variance, a mismatch, an open question.
Red#C1503FSomething is wrong and it will not resolve itself.
The rule

Three states, three colours, no fourth. Every status icon, tag and counter in the module uses exactly these. Amber also carries a darker text version (#9C6A1E) because amber on white is too pale to read as a number.

Where to see it

Screen 6a, Box scanning. The Recent Box Entries table runs green, amber, red down the Status column while the operator scans. Screen 6d uses the darker amber for the Remaining figure, so the one number that matters stands out from the two beside it.

03 — TYPE

Three typefaces, each with a reason

Barlow Semi Condensed for headings, because it is narrow like a number plate and fits a long screen title on a small device. Inter for reading, because it is plain and gets out of the way. IBM Plex Mono for data only — a gate entry number, a box code, a part number.

Unload Truck & Check Boxes
Body text is Inter at 15 to 16 pixels. It is set at a comfortable line height because the people reading it are often reading it quickly, in bad light, with one hand.

The one rule people break

Mono is for data, not for emphasis. If a value could be typed into a search box and found again — a GEN, a box code, a vehicle number — it is mono. If it is a word, it is not.

Where to see it

Screen 4a, Receipt Details. The title is Barlow, the field labels and helper text are Inter, and the Gate Entry Number is Plex Mono. All three families are visible in the top third of the screen.

04 — SPACING & TOUCH

Everything is a multiple of eight

Gaps, padding and margins all come from one ladder built on eight pixels. It sounds arbitrary and it is, but the value of it is that nothing ever sits three pixels off from everything else. On top of that sits one non-negotiable number for the handheld: 44 pixels.

sp-1 · 8px
sp-2 · 16px
sp-3 · 24px
sp-4 · 32px
sp-5 · 48px
sp-6 · 64px
hit-min · 44px
Why 44 pixels

It is the smallest target a person can reliably hit wearing a glove, on a device they are holding in one hand while the other hand is on a carton. Every button, row and toggle on a handheld screen is at least that tall. This is the rule that most often forces a layout to change, and it is the one that never bends.

Where to see it

Screen 6b, Box condition. The six condition options are drawn as large tappable tiles rather than a compact radio list, purely because of the 44-pixel rule. Screen 6f takes it further — the counter's plus button is 76 pixels, because it is pressed several hundred times a shift.

05 — CORNERS

Two radii, and they mean different things

Sharp corners for structure and data — cards, tables, buttons. Soft, fully rounded corners only where the system is telling a person something rather than showing them a number. A rounded pill in this system always means "this is a label about a state", never "this is a control".

Card · 2px Button · 2px Input field · 6px Status tag · 20px
The exception, and why

Input fields use 6 pixels rather than 2. That is not the marketing brand — it was measured off the FRD's own wireframe artwork, so the WMS screens match what the client has already seen and approved.

Where to see it

Screen 6b. The Recommended tag beside Open Delivery is a pill, because it is the system offering an opinion. The Save Condition button next to it is sharp, because it is a control. Same screen, two shapes, two meanings.

06 — DEPTH

Structure is drawn with lines, not shadows

There are no decorative drop shadows anywhere in this system. A card is separated from the page by a one-pixel line and a change of background, not by a glow. Shadow is reserved for things that genuinely float above the screen — a dialog, a dropdown.

LevelValueUsed for
NonenoneCards, panels, tables. The default.
Card0 1px 2px / 6%A card that must lift very slightly off a busy background.
Overlay0 8px 28px / 18%Dialogs and dropdowns only — things that really are in front.
Why it matters on a dock

Shadows wash out in daylight. A one-pixel line does not. On a rugged terminal held outdoors, line-based structure is the only kind that survives.

Where to see it

Any handheld screen. Every panel is a white box with a single hairline border. The only shadow in the whole module is on the explanation dialog that opens when you press an undrawn control in the prototype.

07 — MOTION

Colour changes, nothing moves

Transitions are limited to colour and opacity, at 120 to 280 milliseconds. Nothing bounces, nothing scales on entry, nothing slides in from the side. A button that is pressed goes to 99 percent of its size and back, which reads as a press rather than an animation.

TokenValueUsed for
Fast120msHover and press feedback.
Base180msA panel opening or closing.
Slow280msA dialog appearing. The longest anything is allowed to take.
The reasoning

An operator scanning two hundred boxes will see the same transition two hundred times. Anything with personality becomes an irritation by the fortieth repetition.

Where to see it

Screen 4a, Receipt Details. The Capture Invoice Details accordion opens at 180 milliseconds with no slide and no easing flourish — it is simply open.

08 — GRADIENTS

Depth, never decoration

Three gradients exist. They are for document covers, hero panels and full-screen moments. None of them appears on a control, a button or a card inside the product.

Deep Teal to Soft Teal. The main hero gradient — the teal waking up.
Stone to Cloud. Metal-looking surfaces and quiet dividers.
Deep Teal to Onyx. Once per screen at most, for real weight.
Where to see it

The banner at the top of this page, and the cover of every wireframe document in the portal. Nowhere inside the WMS screens themselves.

09 — ICONS

One set, one weight

All icons come from a single library at a consistent stroke weight of 1.75. They are line icons, never filled, and they sit at 13 to 24 pixels depending on context. An icon never appears alone where it carries meaning — it is always paired with a word.

Flagged substitution

The FRD's own icons exist only as screenshots, so they could not be extracted. The wireframes use Lucide as a stand-in at matching weight. If SpareCare has a licensed icon set, it should replace this before build — the shapes will change, the sizes and rules will not.

Where to see it

Screen 4a. The Gate Entry Number row carries a document icon beside the label. The icon narrows the scan; the word carries the meaning. Remove the word and the row stops working.

10 — VOICE

Talk like the counter, not the boardroom

The audience is store owners, storekeepers and parts managers. Write the way you would talk across a counter: plain, specific, and quick to the point. Say what happened and what to do next.

Do

  • "3 stores near you already have this part."
  • "Your Bay-04 stock hasn't synced since 6pm."
  • Name real parts, brands and store names in examples.
  • Say what happened and what to do next.

Don't

  • "Leverage real-time inventory intelligence."
  • "Unlock synergies across your supply network."
  • Use jargon a counter clerk wouldn't say out loud.
  • Apologise or hedge in an error message — just explain.
Where to see it

Screen 6a. The mismatch warning reads "BX-00019 is not on the uploaded ship list. Set it aside, or add a document to justify accepting it." It names the box, states the problem, and gives the two things the operator can do. No apology, no jargon.

11 — ANNOTATION LAYER

The documentation has its own rules

The numbered circles, the callout boxes and the grey canvas behind every wireframe are not part of the product. They are a separate layer that exists only in these documents, and they are kept visually distinct so nobody mistakes a callout for a control.

ElementMeaning
Numbered circleA design decision, explained in the legend beneath the screen. Every one carries the FRD requirement it comes from.
Open query (amber)The FRD does not answer this. Carries an OI number and must be settled before build.
Our proposal (tinted)A decision we made because the FRD is silent. Accept it or overturn it.
What is deliberately missingStates not drawn and detail not specified, listed at the foot of every screen so the gaps are visible rather than assumed.
The point of it

Every callout carries a traceable reference — an FR number where one exists, a section number otherwise. That traceability is the deliverable. A wireframe without it is a picture; with it, it is a specification you can argue with.

Where to see it

Switch any screen in the left menu to Annotated wireframe. Screen 6g is the clearest example: five numbered decisions, three open queries, and a stated most-important omission.
